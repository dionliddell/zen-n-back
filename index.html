How to play

Zen-n-Back is built to be tough. Every level asks more of your focus and memory than the last, and so far no one in testing, me included, has made it past n = 3. Consider it a challenge.

Tap the draw pile in the centre to start. Each tap turns up a Zener card and deals the previous one onto a random tile around it. If the card n deep in one of the stacks matches the face-up card, tap that stack to score a point and burn it away. If there's no match, tap the draw pile to deal again. Tapping the wrong stack, or dealing when a match was there, costs a point. Every 10 points takes you up a level: fewer tiles, deeper stacks, and more for your memory to hold. The game ends when you reach 50 points or after 5 minutes, and your score is the highest total you reached along the way. Easy mode deals cards face up, Hard mode deals them face down, and training mode shows you where the matches are.

Rules in detail

  • Click the draw pile to start. The clock starts and the first Zener card turns face up.
  • Each click on the draw pile sends the face-up card onto a random tile and turns up the next card.
  • If you think a stack's card n deep matches the face-up card, click that stack instead. At n = 1 that is the top card, at n = 2 the second card down, and so on.
  • Each symbol always has its own colour: star yellow, circle red, waves blue, square black, cross green. A match scores 1 point and burns the face-up card and that whole stack in blue flame.
  • Claiming a stack with no match, or clicking the draw pile when a match was there, costs 1 point and burns every stack and the face-up card. Stacks that did match show their card n deep burning face up. Your score never drops below 0.
  • n rises at 10, 20, 30 and 40 points and falls if you drop below a mark. The board burns clear at each change.
  • Fewer tiles are in play as n rises: all eight at n = 1, the four edges at n = 2, left, top and right at n = 3, and the tiles either side of the draw pile at n = 4 and 5. Unused tiles darken.
  • Double-click the draw pile to undo the last deal. Deal that card again and it returns to the same tile.
  • Easy deals cards face up; Hard deals them face down. Training mode (Hard only) labels the right move with Click Me!, ignores wrong taps (they flash a red cross), goes up a level every 3 points and ends at 15 points or 2 minutes. Training games don't set a high score. To switch either, tap the word on the start or end screen.
  • Your game score is the highest score you reach in 5 minutes, with its n and the time you reached it. Reaching 50 ends the game early. Exit pauses the game, stops the clock and shows Stats. Resume carries on; Reset! ends the game and counts your peak score. After a game, the same button shows Stats.
  • Flames: match, level up, level down, missed match or time up, points target reached (50, or 15 in training).
  • Keys: Space draws. Q W E / A D / Z X C or the number pad claim a stack. Esc closes this panel.

What is Zen-n-Back?

An n-back task shows you a stream of items and asks whether the current one matches the one from n steps earlier. Psychologists have used it since the 1950s to measure working memory, the mental scratchpad you use to hold and update information.

As a training exercise, n-back is said to strengthen working memory, attention and focus, and some early studies suggested gains in reasoning too. The evidence is mixed: people reliably get better at the task itself, while benefits beyond it are still debated.

I've always wanted to combine n-back with a card game, and Zen-n-Back, made in partnership with AI, is the result.

- Dion Liddell, Claude
